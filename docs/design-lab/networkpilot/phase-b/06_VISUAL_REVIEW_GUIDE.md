# Phase B Visual Review Guide

This package reviews the isolated Network Command prototype at `/design-lab/network-command`. Every visible person, company, message, count, and provider condition is deterministic fictional prototype data.

Contact sheets:

- `artifacts/design-lab/network-command/desktop-contact-sheet.png`
- `artifacts/design-lab/network-command/mobile-contact-sheet.png`

## Desktop

### 01 — Today

- **Shows:** Ordered work under Now with a quieter Live State rail.
- **Demonstrates:** Work-first command-center composition without KPI cards.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the selected task is dominant enough, the live rail recedes appropriately, and the first viewport feels immediately actionable.

### 02 — Drafts, 5 items

- **Shows:** The joined queue, message, inspector, and action dock with a short queue.
- **Demonstrates:** The core composition when every draft can remain visible.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the empty queue area feels intentional, the selected person remains grounded, and the message plane carries enough visual weight.

### 03 — Drafts, 15 items

- **Shows:** Normal working density across all four lifecycle groups.
- **Demonstrates:** The primary NetworkPilot review loop at its expected daily scale.
- **Look critically at:** Selection strength, row rhythm, message readability, inspector hierarchy, and action-dock balance.

### 04 — Drafts, 30 items

- **Shows:** High queue density at a 1024 × 768 viewport.
- **Demonstrates:** Long-title truncation and continuity under constrained width.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the inspector becomes too compressed, metadata remains legible, and the message still feels like the primary surface.

### 05 — Drafts, approved state

- **Shows:** An approved immutable draft with Create Gmail Draft as the next lifecycle action.
- **Demonstrates:** State-specific action language and immutable-version context.
- **Look critically at:** Whether Approved is unmistakable without excessive color and whether the next action feels appropriately consequential.

### 06 — Drafts, uncertain-send state

- **Shows:** An unresolved send operation retained in the queue.
- **Demonstrates:** Caution without implying failure or success.
- **Look critically at:** Whether uncertainty is prominent enough, retry risk is clear, and Resolve Send Status is differentiated from normal lifecycle progress.

### 07 — Command palette

- **Shows:** Navigation and context commands over the Drafts workspace.
- **Demonstrates:** A momentary high-contrast command surface with explanations for unavailable commands.
- **Look critically at:** Whether it feels integrated with the light product, teaches capability clearly, and avoids terminal or generic launcher aesthetics.

### 08 — Candidates, comfortable density

- **Shows:** Identity-first candidate list with selected-person inspector.
- **Demonstrates:** Abundant opportunity without cards or bulk controls.
- **Look critically at:** Whether people remain more prominent than metadata and whether the inspector makes the list feel exploratory rather than administrative.

### 09 — Candidates, compact density

- **Shows:** The same supply surface at higher information density.
- **Demonstrates:** Density as a deliberate mode rather than accidental compression.
- **Look critically at:** Scanability, row separation, selected-state strength, and whether compact mode starts to feel like a database.

### 10 — Apollo exhausted

- **Shows:** Candidate refresh constrained while existing work remains available.
- **Demonstrates:** Provider failure semantics that do not visually disable unrelated work.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the consequence is clear without dominating Today and whether the repair timing is understandable.

### 11 — Gmail reconnect required

- **Shows:** Approved draft retained with Gmail creation disabled and a repair action.
- **Demonstrates:** A truthful adjacent disabled reason and scoped provider blockage.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the reason is readable without crowding the dock and whether Reconnect Gmail competes appropriately with the disabled lifecycle action.

### 12 — Send confirmation

- **Shows:** Explicit confirmation before the simulated lifecycle transfer.
- **Demonstrates:** Modal elevation reserved for consequential action and a visible zero-provider-call boundary.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the hierarchy communicates consequence, whether the simulation status is impossible to miss, and whether the dialog feels too generic.

### 13 — Send success

- **Shows:** Queue continuation with Sent incremented and a compact confirmation event.
- **Demonstrates:** Lifecycle feedback without confetti or navigation interruption.
- **Look critically at:** Whether causality is understandable from the event line and whether success feedback is visible enough without obscuring the next draft.

## Mobile

### 14 — Today

- **Shows:** Ordered Now feed, selected work, compressed Live State, and bottom navigation.
- **Demonstrates:** A mobile composition that begins with work rather than a reduced desktop dashboard.
- **Look critically at:** Whether work priority survives the narrow viewport and whether Live State arrives at the right point in the scroll.

### 15 — Draft queue

- **Shows:** State counts, Add Drafts, grouped rows, selected person, and bottom navigation.
- **Demonstrates:** Queue-first mobile entry with preserved lifecycle context.
- **Look critically at:** Row density, long-title truncation, selection strength, and whether top controls feel crowded.

### 16 — Draft review

- **Shows:** Full-screen recipient, attachment, subject, body, evidence disclosure, position, and sticky actions.
- **Demonstrates:** Independent mobile review rather than horizontally compressed desktop panes.
- **Look critically at:** Reading measure, subject truncation, sticky-action balance, evidence discoverability, and the amount of chrome around the message.

### 17 — Candidates

- **Shows:** Search, Filter, identity-first rows, availability, lane, and bottom navigation.
- **Demonstrates:** Opportunity browsing with a compact mobile information hierarchy.
- **Look critically at:** Whether role and company order is natural, right-aligned metadata remains calm, and the surface avoids database-table character.

### 18 — Filter sheet

- **Shows:** Track, lane, and region filters in a bottom sheet with Done and Clear.
- **Demonstrates:** Structured filtering without five permanent dropdowns.
- **Look critically at:** Whether the sheet is easy to dismiss, control spacing supports touch, and the underlying candidate context remains useful.

## Open visual questions

- Is the design distinctive enough from Linear or Superhuman?
- Does the light palette feel premium or too clinical?
- Does Drafts feel tactile enough?
- Is person identity strong enough?
- Is the inspector too dense at medium width?
- Does Candidates feel like opportunity rather than a database?
- Does Today feel like a command center rather than a task list?
- Does mobile feel independently composed?
- Does the command palette feel integrated or bolted on?
- Does the system still feel too AI-generated?
- Is the selected-row treatment recognizable across long sessions without becoming visually tiring?
- Is the action dock sufficiently consequential without encouraging throughput over judgment?
- Do Gmail, Apollo, uncertain-send, and success states feel semantically different before reading their text?
